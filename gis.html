If vector-based GIS over-determines the geographic world by forcing it into a uniform mold of geometric objects, the raster-based alternative is guilty of feigning maximal ignorance as to the nature of things in that world.
Features may be purely accidental clusters or patterns of values, they may be unstable at the relevant time-scales, they may or may not be bounded in Euclidean space, they may be part of an object, or themselves contain several objects. This fleeting nature of "features" is in stark contrast with the strong individuality of "objects" in vector-based GIS, which are identified and defined as discrete, localized individuals prior to any attributes they may possess.
